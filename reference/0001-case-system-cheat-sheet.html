The case system

Cases mark the role of a noun phrase, so Polish can show who does what to whom without relying on fixed word order.

01Choose a case in six steps

  1. Find the noun phraseWhich person, thing, place, or idea are you changing?
  2. Check the prepositiondo, w, z, o, etc. often select the case.
  3. Check the verbSome verbs require a case or fixed construction.
  4. Find its roleSubject, object, recipient, companion, location, addressee?
  5. Select the caseUse the map below. Meaning comes before endings.
  6. Select the endingUse gender, number, and the noun's final sound. Later lessons teach this.

03Trigger bank

Sentence role

  • subject → Mianownik
  • direct object → usually Biernik
  • recipient → Celownik

Prepositions

  • do + Dopełniacz
  • w + Miejscownik (static location)
  • w + Biernik (movement into)
  • z + Narzędnik (with)
  • z + Dopełniacz (from)
  • o + Miejscownik (about)

Verb constructions

  • czekać na + Biernik
  • uczyć się + Dopełniacz
  • pomagać + Celownik

Common traps

  • Choose the role and case before thinking about the ending.
  • A noun can look unchanged: chleb is both Mianownik and Biernik.
  • The same preposition can select different cases when its meaning changes.
  • "Looks like Genitive" does not mean "is Genitive." Masculine animate Biernik often copies its form.
